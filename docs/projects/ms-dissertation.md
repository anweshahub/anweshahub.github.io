## Overview

This dissertation investigated how plants at different ends of the stress-tolerance spectrum regulate their stress-responsive genes at the **promoter level**. By comparing two species — the highly stress-tolerant *Thellungiella salsuginea* and the stress-sensitive model plant *Arabidopsis thaliana* — the project aimed to identify promoter-level regulatory differences that may explain their contrasting abilities to cope with osmotic stress.

---

## Gene Families Studied

Four gene families with known stress-responsive roles in Brassicaceae were selected:

| Gene Family | Role |
|---|---|
| **DREB1** | Dehydration-Responsive Element Binding factors — cold and drought response |
| **SWEET** | Sugar Will Eventually be Exported Transporters — osmotic and biotic stress |
| **LIM** | LIM-domain proteins — cytoskeletal remodelling under stress |
| **ANNEXIN** | Calcium-dependent membrane-binding proteins — abiotic stress signalling |

---

## Study Species

- ***Thellungiella salsuginea*** (salt cress) — extreme halophyte and drought-tolerant species; a natural model for stress tolerance in Brassicaceae
- ***Arabidopsis thaliana*** — well-characterized stress-sensitive model plant

---

## Methods

- Retrieval of promoter sequences (upstream regulatory regions) from public genome databases
- Identification and comparative analysis of **cis-regulatory elements** within promoters
- Statistical analysis of element frequency differences between the two species
- Functional annotation of discovered motifs using plant transcription factor databases

---

## Key Questions Addressed

1. Do stress-tolerant *T. salsuginea* and stress-sensitive *A. thaliana* differ in the types or density of cis-regulatory elements in the promoters of these four gene families?
2. Which regulatory motifs may confer superior stress responsiveness in *T. salsuginea*?
3. Can promoter architecture alone explain part of the phenotypic difference in osmotic stress tolerance between the two species?
