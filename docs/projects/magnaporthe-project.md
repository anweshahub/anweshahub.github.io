## Background

*Magnaporthe oryzae* is the causal agent of rice blast, one of the most devastating fungal diseases of rice globally. A key virulence mechanism of this pathogen is the secretion of **effector proteins** into host cells — small molecules that manipulate host immunity and facilitate infection. Understanding these effectors at the structural and biophysical level is essential for developing durable resistance strategies.

---

## Study A — Secretome-Wide Intrinsic Disorder Analysis

**Scale:** ~365 small secretory effector molecules from the complete *M. oryzae* secretome

**Bioinformatic pipeline:**

1. Identified putative effectors using criteria: presence of a signal peptide, absence of transmembrane domains, and confirmed signal peptide cleavage site
2. Used **FoldIndex** to quantify intrinsic disorder, infoldability, charge, and hydrophobicity for all 365 effectors
3. Classified effectors into six structural categories:

| Class | Description |
|---|---|
| Ordered | Predominantly structured proteins |
| Mostly ordered | Slight tendency toward flexibility |
| Moderately disordered | Mixed structure–disorder |
| Mostly disordered | Largely unstructured |
| Highly disordered | Minimal structured regions |
| Extremely disordered | Almost entirely unstructured |

**Significance:** Intrinsic disorder in effectors is functionally important — disordered regions enable binding to multiple host targets, facilitate translocation, and confer adaptability to host immune pressure.

---

## Study B — Molecular-Level Characterization of Three Hypothetical Effectors

Three hypothetical effector genes were selected for in-depth analysis:

- Natural intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs)
- Percentage disorder across the full sequence
- **Phosphorylation sites** in the amino acid sequence
- Possible protein domains and their predicted functions
- Post-translational modification (PTM) sites
