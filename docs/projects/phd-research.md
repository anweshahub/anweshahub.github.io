## Research Overview

This PhD project sits at the convergence of material science, plant biology, and environmental microbiology. The central material is **biochar** — a carbon-rich product formed by the thermal degradation (slow pyrolysis) of organic feedstocks such as aquatic weeds or agricultural waste — and the overarching question is how its incorporation into soil reshapes plant growth and the microbial community that lives within it.

---

## Research Aims

### 1. Biochar Synthesis
Synthesizing biochar via **slow pyrolysis** from:

- Aquatic weeds (locally abundant, problematic biomass)
- Agricultural waste residues

Characterizing the resulting material for surface properties, carbon content, and functional groups relevant to soil–plant–microbe interactions.

### 2. Soil Amendment & Crop Growth
Applying biochar as a **soil amendment** and tracking its effects on crop growth at two levels:

- **Phenotypic:** plant height, biomass, root morphology, yield
- **Genotypic:** expression of developmental genes involved in growth promotion — tracing phenotypic outcomes to their molecular basis

> **Current result:** Biochar derived from aquatic weeds shows a statistically significant positive impact on mustard (*Brassica juncea*) plant growth. This work is being prepared for publication.

### 3. Soil Microbial Community Dynamics
Studying how prolonged biochar exposure in the soil shifts the native microbial community:

- Community profiling at **genus and species level**
- Identifying taxa that increase or decrease in abundance under biochar treatment
- Hypothesis: biochar acts as a selective habitat, favouring electroactive and carbon-utilizing microorganisms

### 4. Extracellular Electron Transfer (EET) — Quantum-Level Interactions
The most novel component of this work: modelling the **electronic communication** between biochar surfaces and soil microbes using **DFT (Density Functional Theory)** simulations.

- Simulating electronic signatures at the **carbon surface–cell wall/protein interface**
- Understanding the quantum-level basis of EET between biochar and microbes
- This layer of analysis links material chemistry directly to microbial physiology
